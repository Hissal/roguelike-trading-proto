Quarterly reports and the news feed

The question: what do a company's quarterly report and news look like, and what does each do to the market? Reports no longer have to show the exact worth. Each field tells one tense: Result is today's verdict on the price, Revenue the past, Profit the present, and Outlook the future: it sets which way the company drifts until the next report. The price move after the report tells you the rest. News is either theoretical (talk, forecasts, "could"; it fades) or concrete (it happened; the next report shows it). You tell them apart by the wording; a card could reveal the kind outright. Developing stories are expected news: an event with a known date and three possible endings (good, neutral, bad). The odds lean on the company's outlook and on the hints so far. On the day, the price jumps by the gap between the value the crowd had priced in and the value the ending actually delivers. One day is one tick; a month is 20 days.

Which rules make free money?

Plays many months without you and trades $200 by rule, at the price just after each item, closing after the hold. Follow all news: buy every ▲, short every ▼. The theoretical-only and concrete-only rows show the edge from knowing an item's kind. Follow the reports: buy a beat, short a miss. Follow the outlook: buy on Raised, short on Cut. Story endings, follow the lean: the day before an ending, bet the way the odds bar leans. Story endings, against the crowd: bet the way the price is wrong: long if the crowd priced in less than the odds suggest, short if more. Random is the baseline. A good design: simple rules win a little, reading the situation wins more, and nothing is a sure thing.
