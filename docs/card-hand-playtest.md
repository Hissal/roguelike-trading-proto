# Card-hand playtest and market direction

Source: creator feedback after playing `card-hand-prototype.html` on September
26, 2026. These are reported experiences, not observed telemetry.

The creator found the card version better, but often saw no useful reason to
play cards. Concentrating in an obviously rising stock and boosting it remained
the apparent best strategy. Investigate, Influence, and Extend supported that
position; Echo on another asset offered little reason to divide capital.
Suppress was the notable exception: pushing a prospective purchase down while
invested elsewhere created preparation across assets.

The creator rejected making concentrated rotation the central game. Repeating
all-in, prepare, sell, and swap felt binary and boring. Concentration and holding
several assets should both be viable in suitable circumstances, with dangers.
The existing market felt safe, predictable, and almost exclusively upward or
downward; even an exit on the first falling tick retained considerable profit.

Candidate directions include partial capital commitments for later benefits,
dividends that create a reason to hold through risk, independently arriving news,
and initial selloffs that can reverse when the underlying situation is clarified.

## Authorized next experiment

The creator authorized the proposed uncertain-market experiment: staggered news,
multiple seeded follow-throughs, consequential moves before a reactive exit,
and explicit public uncertainty. Keep cards/accounting and test whether position
size and holding duration become meaningful. See
`../.scratch/uncertain-market/spec.md`. No design success is established yet.

## Rival candidate

The creator proposed beating a rival's profit instead of a flat profit target.
The rival's evolving profit would create a dynamic, not fully known target.
Visible holdings or next moves could give the player reasons to use cards on
unowned assets and combine offense with defense.

This is recorded as a separate next-experiment candidate, not implemented in the
market-only pass. Open questions include what rival information is public,
whether intentions are commitments, how the rival reacts, action order, and how
profit is compared at closing. A rival should create meaningful opposing
interests rather than merely provide a moving number to chase.
