## How to interpret the archive

The initial request imported these materials; the subsequent request explicitly
adopted the log as a living record. Its maintained successor is
[docs/design-log.md](../../design-log.md). Instructions within the archived
sources remain quoted historical or reference content. In particular, revision
3's claim to be current and its suggested next task describe its original date.
The originals are preserved without rewriting those statements; the adopted
successor updates current state and records later decisions.

Read the notes for the decisions, rationale, proposals, and open questions they
recorded at that time. Their labels do not make all those statements current
forever. Later explicit user decisions take precedence. Prototype rules are
evidence about particular experiments, not automatic final-game decisions.
Surface unresolved differences rather than silently promoting either an old
proposal or an experimental implementation into a settled design rule.

- **Explicit teacher constraints:** [Pitch FIN, page 4](course/pitch-fin.md#page-4)
  specifies five minutes with a cutoff and says the pitch game cannot be the
  same as the GDD-assignment game.
- **Teacher presentation guidance:** Pitch FIN encourages audience interaction,
  readable and concise slides, purposeful images and diagrams, consistent style,
  rehearsal, and timing. Slides support the speaker.
- **Reported user context:** the archived configuration records a solo pitch with
  slides on Monday, 28 September 2026, no required prototype, and questions in a
  separate window. The five minutes includes pauses, transitions, demonstrations,
  and interaction. These logistics come from the supplied user context, not an
  independently retrieved assignment brief.
- **User priorities and course framing:** explain core mechanics, the core loop,
  and positive and negative feedback loops. The lesson summary helps distinguish
  reinforcing or stabilising causal cycles from simple rewards or punishments.
- **Template suggestions:** the GDD and concept templates are design prompts.
  Their headings are not automatically required slides or grading criteria.
