## Accepted decisions and rationale

### 1. Independent, accessible market

- Ordinary trades do not influence prices by default. Specific upgrades can provide price influence as a build direction.
- The core experience is reading a situation and deciding how to act, with understandable signals rather than extensive required financial knowledge.
- Signals should be substantially easier to read than real-world markets. Consistency lets experienced players infer relationships and choose other upgrades instead of interpretation aids.
- Public evidence must support meaningful decisions before information upgrades. Timing, magnitude, and competing influences can preserve uncertainty despite understandable event effects.
- Market correction is baseline behaviour: prices tend toward a changing underlying value rather than instantly matching it.
- Exact underlying value is not automatically visible. Estimating it gives analysis and information abilities something useful to reveal.
- Identifying undervaluation differs from knowing that the price will rise before closing. Value and timing are distinct uncertainties.

Rationale: preserve the feeling of clever, explainable trading while allowing stronger tools and supernatural advantages to develop across a run.

### 2. Guaranteed progression and useful knowledge

- Players must receive upgrades without paying from trading profits.
- Knowledge can include trading capabilities, analysis, and specialisations, not merely additional asset permissions.
- Reduced broker fees and other gamified trading benefits fit. Realistic inspiration is useful, but fictional justification is acceptable where it supports the game.
- Analysis upgrades can clarify news implications, affected companies, or credibility. They may make public evidence easier to interpret without making the same inference impossible for an experienced player.

Working delivery direction, not final rules: XP or similar progression grants knowledge choices; profits buy additional advantages, particularly supernatural cards. One-of-three choices were illustrative. The original one-free-upgrade-between-days model is a fallback baseline, not an automatically additional reward alongside XP.

Alternatives preserved: a separate upgrade resource, possibly purchasable with profits; final XP sources and cadence remain undecided.

### 3. Supernatural deck alongside ordinary trading

- Draw-dependent tools should encourage improvisation even after the player develops a strategy.
- Basic buying and selling remain available without drawing action cards.
- Keep the playable deck supernatural. Ordinary advantages come through news, knowledge, insider tips, and other ordinary means.
- Ordinary runs begin with a very small functional set of cards.
- The shady shop is the primary deck-customisation source, using profits. Encounters and especially bosses can also provide cards.
- A predetermined introductory tutorial run teaches basic trading first, then introduces a few cards near its end for players to try.

Rationale: permanent trading competence supports a build, while a changing hand produces daily adaptation. Starting cards ensure supernatural play does not depend entirely on spare profits; tutorial sequencing avoids introducing everything simultaneously.

Proposals still open: tarot-like presentation, displayed shop offers, rerolls, card removal, exact prices, starter deck, and card effects.

### 4. Information channels

Accepted distinction:
- Analysis: infer from public evidence.
- Insider tip: receive a specific nonpublic fact through an ordinary source.
- Prophecy: gain knowledge unavailable through ordinary means.

Mechanical effect and fictional source are separate: all three can provide information. Experience can substitute for interpreting public evidence, but not for access to genuinely unavailable facts. Credible information does not guarantee a price outcome when other influences remain.

Contacts, relationships, and spying were discussed as possible framing or future systems, not approved additional progression systems.

### 5. Feedback loops

Positive direction: profitable trades → more capital and purchasing power → larger positions and stronger purchased tools → greater potential profits → more capital. This is potential earning power, not guaranteed success.

Negative market loop: price above underlying value → stronger corrective selling pressure → price moves toward value → correction weakens. Below value, corrective buying pressure can operate in the opposite direction. This regulates prices, not necessarily player wealth.

Negative rival loop: takeover threat rises → rival strengthens defence/interference → progress is checked or pushed back → threat falls → defensive pressure eases. Exact threat measures and actions remain open. Scheduled escalation alone does not establish this loop.

Recovery direction: financial stress rises → conditional assistance becomes more available/effective → successful use improves finances → stress falls → assistance recedes. Certain upgrades, news, and encounters can provide support; this is not a blanket rule that poverty grants more XP.

A hidden helper is acceptable in principle. Accepted standard: adjust access to useful opportunities/information while preserving established market rules. Do not secretly make a purchased asset rise simply because the player is struggling. Agency remains in recognising and using an opportunity.

Pitch direction: foreground profit-driven growth and responsive rival resistance; acknowledge market correction as normal market behaviour and keep recovery support secondary. Exact final diagrams and examples are unfinished.

## Explicitly deferred decisions

The user stopped premature specification of card circulation. These require prototype evidence and are not necessary to settle before the pitch:
- Does an empty draw pile reshuffle the discard pile? Are any cards exhausted for the day?
- Does each turn replace the hand? Are unplayed cards discarded or retained?
- For real time, are draws timed, cards on timers, or hands manually refreshed/discarded?
- What is the maximum hand size and overflow behaviour?
- How can the player draw additional cards, and at what cost?
- What resets between days, and how do upgrades modify these rules?

The assistant's opening-hand, timed/phase-draw, retention, and once-per-day suggestions were not accepted. Neither real time nor turn based was selected.

Also open: exact XP sources and upgrade economy; market equations and assets; card roster; boss encounter timing; acquisition mechanics; alternate destruction conditions and rewards; run length; financial-collapse definition; classes and metaprogression; final title, visual/audio direction, and pitch production choices.

## Illustrations, not settled mechanics

- A factory interruption lowers price; replacement parts are expected with uncertain timing; privileged information confirms arrival before closing. The player chooses early purchase, partial entry, waiting, or another opportunity.
- Shorting knowledge plus event analysis plus prophecy illustrates cooperation between permanent expertise and drawn powers.
- Lower fees under financial stress illustrate a conditional recovery upgrade.
- Holding-related profit benefits and diversification protection were possible specialisations, not final upgrade designs.
- Real-world broker activity tiers inspired fee specialisations. Provider-specific pricing was not verified or adopted as game rules.

## Risks identified for later testing

- Guaranteed recovery timing could make buying and waiting too obvious.
- XP per transaction could incentivise meaningless trades; profit-based XP could compound success excessively.
- Useful supernatural play should not be inaccessible to players without spare profits.
- Card management may distract from reading the market, especially in real time.
- Rivals should respond to threat without making success feel arbitrarily punished; instantaneous buyouts might bypass that response.
- Recovery assistance should not remove player agency or contradict observable market rules.
